# Cover letter (draft — to be signed by the corresponding author)

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript **"Resonant versus geometric anisotropy of the Purcell enhancement near a gold nanorod: an equal-volume-sphere control and design rules for plasmon-enhanced single-photon sources"** for consideration in *[journal]*.

The emission rate of a quantum emitter near an elongated plasmonic particle depends strongly on the orientation of its transition dipole. This anisotropy is routinely reported, but it has not been established how much of it is the generic near-surface effect present for any metal body and how much is specific to the longitudinal resonance of the elongated particle. The distinction matters because randomly oriented emitters and orientation-averaged models remove exactly the resonant part.

We resolve this question by introducing an **equal-volume gold sphere as a control** and probing both particles with two dipole orientations at an identical 5-nm gap, with a free-space reference and validation against exact Mie theory. The sphere's orientation ratio remains between 2.2 and 2.7 over the visible and near infrared, the quasi-static near-surface value, and the nanorod shows the same value off resonance; at the longitudinal resonance the nanorod ratio reaches 41 for the total decay rate and 358 for the radiated power. Including the emitter's intrinsic quantum yield, we further show that **the optimal emitter–tip gap for a single-photon source depends on the quantum yield**: 5–10 nm for poor emitters, 20 nm for near-unity emitters.

The work combines full three-dimensional FDTD with a body-of-revolution solver that allows sub-nanometre grids, and reports a complete uncertainty budget. All raw spectra, scripts and analysis code are openly available.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors approved the submission. We declare no competing interests.
