\section*{S2. Plasmon-eigenmode decomposition, excitation and additional set-ups}
\emph{Eigenmodes.} The 40 lowest eigenvalues of the quasistatic BEM matrix $F$ were computed with
\texttt{bemstateig} of MNPBEM17 on the \SI{2}{nm} meshes (sphere: 784 vertices). The longitudinal (transverse)
dipolar mode is the eigenvector $u_k$ with the largest oscillator strength $s_k=(\textstyle\sum_j a_j\mathbf r_j u_{k,j})\cdot(\tilde u_k\cdot\mathbf n)$
for a uniform field along $z$ ($x$), where $a_j$, $\mathbf r_j$ and $\mathbf n_j$ are the area, centroid and normal
of element $j$; its contribution to $\Fp-1$ follows from Eq.~(3) of the main text and is evaluated with the same
decay-rate routine as the full solution. The trivial eigenvalue $-2\pi$ (uniform charge) does not couple. Script:
\texttt{isi/bem/extra/job\_modes.m}. Table~\ref{tab:modes} lists the decomposition at the resonance of the
longitudinal mode.

\emph{Collection on glass by reciprocity.} The angular emission of the dipole next to the rod on glass was obtained
from $\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}\Omega=n_j\sum_{\rm pol}|\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf E_{\rm loc}(\hat{\mathbf s},{\rm pol})|^2$,
where $\mathbf E_{\rm loc}$ is the field at the dipole for a unit-amplitude plane wave incident from direction
$\hat{\mathbf s}$ in medium $j$ (air or glass), computed with the layer BEM for $24\times36$ directions per half-space
and two polarisations (3456 right-hand sides of one BEM solution; \texttt{job\_recip.m}). A free dipole gives
$8\pi/3$, which fixes the absolute normalisation. The formula was verified against an independent angular-spectrum
(Fresnel) calculation for a bare dipole above glass (\texttt{isi/analysis/glass\_check.py}). The far field returned
directly by the layer routines of MNPBEM17 was not used: the induced-charge and dipole contributions are returned on
differently ordered direction sets, so that their sum, and quantities derived from it, are inconsistent for weakly
radiating dipoles. Table~\ref{tab:recip} lists the checks and results.

\emph{Nonlocal response.} Following Luo \emph{et al.}~[8] the metal is shrunk by $\Delta d=\SI{0.05}{nm}$ and
covered by a layer of the same thickness with permittivity
$\varepsilon_t=\varepsilon_m\varepsilon_b q_L\Delta d/(\varepsilon_m-\varepsilon_b)$,
$q_L=\sqrt{\omega_p^2/\varepsilon_\infty-\omega(\omega+i\gamma)}/\beta$, with the tabulated Johnson--Christy
$\varepsilon_m$, $\hbar\omega_p=\SI{9.02}{eV}$, $\varepsilon_\infty=9.84$, $\hbar\gamma=\SI{0.071}{eV}$ and
$\beta=\sqrt{3/5}\,v_F$, $v_F=\SI{1.40e6}{m/s}$; integration over neighbouring elements of the two surfaces uses
polar refinement (\texttt{npol}~=~20). The local reference uses the same mesh. Script: \texttt{job\_nonlocal.m}.

\section*{S3. Drude--Lorentz representation of Johnson--Christy gold}
For the time-domain body-of-revolution solver the Johnson--Christy permittivity~[1] is represented as
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon(f)=\varepsilon_\infty-\frac{\sigma_0 f_0^2}{f^2+i f\gamma_0}
+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\sigma_j f_j^2}{f_j^2-f^2-i f\gamma_j},
\end{equation}
with $f=1/\lambda$ in \si{\micro\metre^{-1}} (Meep convention, $e^{-i\omega t}$). A least-squares fit over
\SIrange{440}{1000}{nm}, weighted by $|\varepsilon|^{-1/2}$, gives the parameters of Table~\ref{tab:fit}; the
root-mean-square relative error is \SI{1.6}{\percent} and the maximum \SI{3.4}{\percent}. The same model is used in
the Mie reference calculations, so that benchmark differences reflect discretisation only.

\section*{S6. Body-of-revolution solver: method and benchmarks}
Cylindrical-coordinate FDTD (Meep~[2]) with field dependence $e^{im\varphi}$; axial dipole: $E_z$ source, $m=0$;
transverse dipole: $E_r$ source, $m=1$. $T$ is the ratio of the fluxes through a closed surface (top and bottom discs and a cylindrical side wall) around
the particle with and without it. $F_p=(P_{\rm rad}+P_{\rm abs})/P_0$, where $P_{\rm abs}$ is the net inflow through a
second closed surface that encloses only the particle, its top disc lying at mid-gap. The local density of states
at the source cell gives the same order of magnitude but is spiky near the metal and is used only as a cross-check. The band
\SIrange{490}{900}{nm} is assembled from three runs with pulses covering \SIrange{460}{610}{}, \SIrange{540}{760}{}
and \SIrange{630}{990}{nm}, of which only \SIrange{490}{570}{}, \SIrange{570}{700}{} and \SIrange{700}{900}{nm} are
kept: a broadband single run produced errors up to \SI{37}{\percent} at the band edges, where the source spectrum
is weak. Run time 30~\si{\micro\metre}/$c$ after the source (identical $F_p$ to 60); PML thickness
\SI{0.15}{\micro\metre}, with a \SI{0.3}{\micro\metre} check at the finest grid. To avoid long-lived numerical ringing,
the Lorentz-pole damping in the gold fit is bounded below by \SI{0.2}{\micro\metre^{-1}} (same fit accuracy).

Benchmark against the exact Mie solution for a dielectric sphere ($\varepsilon=4$, $R=\SI{15.874}{nm}$, radial dipole
at \SI{5}{nm}): a grid of \SI{2}{nm} reproduces $F_p$ within \SI{1}{\percent} in the band centres; a \SI{4}{nm} grid
gives a uniform \SIrange{4}{5}{\percent} overestimate with a maximum deviation of \SI{12}{\percent}. For gold (radial dipole, \SI{5}{nm} gap) at a \SI{2}{nm} grid, $T$ deviates from Mie by about
\SI{10}{\percent} while the power-balance $F_p$ is about three times too large; at a \SI{20}{nm} gap the $F_p$
error drops to \SI{24}{\percent} and $T$ agrees within \SI{5}{\percent}. The $F_p$ error is therefore a near-field
discretisation error of the staircased metal surface. Grids of 2, 1.33 and \SI{1}{nm} with linear extrapolation to
zero spacing are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:bor}.

\section*{S7. Semi-analytic spheroid solution and closed-form single-mode model}
\emph{Multipole solution.} For a prolate spheroid with semi-axes $a>b$ (focal distance $f=\sqrt{a^2-b^2}$, surface
$\xi=\xi_0=a/f$ in prolate spheroidal coordinates $\xi,\eta,\varphi$) and a dipole on the axis at $z_0>a$, the potential
inside is $\sum_n A_nP_n^m(\xi)P_n^m(\eta)\cos m\varphi$ and the induced potential outside
$\sum_n B_nQ_n^m(\xi)P_n^m(\eta)\cos m\varphi$, with $m=0$ for the axial and $m=1$ for the transverse dipole~[9]. The
incident coefficients $s_n=c_nP_n^m(\xi_0)$ are obtained by projecting the dipole potential on the surface onto
$P_n^m(\eta)$ (1500-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature); continuity of the potential and of $\varepsilon\,\partial_\xi\phi$
gives
\begin{equation}
B_nQ_n^m(\xi_0)=\frac{(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_b)\,P_n'/P_n}{\varepsilon_b\,Q_n'/Q_n-\varepsilon\,P_n'/P_n}\,s_n ,
\end{equation}
where the logarithmic derivatives are evaluated at $\xi_0$ (type-3 Legendre functions, 30-digit arithmetic), so that
only ratios of Legendre functions enter. The induced field at the dipole follows by differentiating the series
($n\leq50$; converged to $<10^{-6}$ at a \SI{5}{nm} gap), $\Fp=1+\tfrac{3}{2k^3}\mathrm{Im}\,(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf E_{\rm ind})$
and $T=|1+p_{\rm ind}|^2$, where $p_{\rm ind}$ is the dipole moment of the $n=1$ term. For the $n=1$ terms the
radiation reaction $i\tfrac23k^3p_{\rm ind}$ and the dynamic depolarisation $k^2p_{\rm ind}/a$ act as a uniform
field on the particle and are included self-consistently. The code is \texttt{isi/analysis/spheroid\_qs.py}.

\emph{Closed form.} Keeping the $n=1$, $m=0$ term only, the field of the dipolar mode on the axis per unit dipole
moment is $E_1(z_0)=-(3/f^3)\,Q_1'(\xi)$ with $Q_1(\xi)=\tfrac{\xi}{2}\ln\frac{\xi+1}{\xi-1}-1$, which gives
Eq.~(5) of the main text; by reciprocity the same factor describes the excitation of the mode by the emitter. The
polarisability of the mode at its resonance is approximated by the peak of the quasistatic Lorentzian with
$1/L_z=1-\varepsilon'$, combined with radiation damping. For a rod, the spheroid with the same half-length and apex
radius of curvature ($b^2/a=D/2$) is used for $E_1$, and the actual rod volume for $\alpha_L$. All 30 configurations
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:law}.

\section*{S10. Comparison with Khatua \emph{et al.}: surface damping and substrate}
The rods of the comparison with Khatua \emph{et al.} ($D=\SI{25}{nm}$, $L=39$--\SI{70}{nm}) were recomputed (i) in
homogeneous glycerol ($n=1.47$) with surface-damped gold (Section~S8) and (ii) lying on glass ($n=1.52$), \SI{1}{nm}
above it and covered by glycerol, with the dipole beyond the tip on the axis and parallel to it and excitation through
the substrate at normal incidence, polarised along the rod (layer Green function, MNPBEM). Rates are normalised to bulk
glycerol and $E_{\rm exc}$ to the field at the same position without the rod. Figure~\ref{fig:khvar} compares the three
models with the measurements. Surface damping lowers the maximum enhancement from 903 to 674 (\SI{5}{nm} gap) and from
\num{1.32e3} to 960 (\SI{3}{nm}); the substrate changes it by $+2$ and $+\SI{6}{\percent}$. For all three models the
ratio of the measured to the calculated enhancement has the same spread (rms of $\log_{10}$ 0.29--0.33 at \SI{3}{nm}
and 0.38--0.44 at \SI{5}{nm} under \SI{633}{nm} excitation; 0.39--0.54 under \SI{594}{nm}), i.e.\ neither effect
reproduces the broader measured dependence on the resonance wavelength. Script: \texttt{isi/analysis/khatua\_variants.py}.
